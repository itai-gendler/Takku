# The Summary reads the Transcript as Markdown; WebVTT, a cleaned Transcript and a check pass were tried

The Summary request carries the Transcript as it is in the Vault: `**[mm:ss] Speaker N:** text`, one paragraph per block. Three ways to make it more accurate, above all for small local models, were looked at on 2026-10-15, on one real Meeting: a 73-minute Italian retrospective, about 8,700 words in 140 paragraphs (about 18,000 tokens by the prompt's estimate), with nine voices. Every Summary was checked claim by claim against the Transcript.

**Cleaning the Transcript** before the request was measured and dropped: filler words ("diciamo", "magari", "cioè"…) are about 3% of the words, the `**[mm:ss] Speaker N:**` labels about 6% of the characters, and 16 of the 140 paragraphs have four words or fewer ("Ok.", "Mm-hmm."). Removing all of it saves about a tenth of the tokens and can drop a short "yes" that agrees to a decision.

**WebVTT** was tried in a Debug build: each paragraph became a cue ending where the next one starts, with the Speaker as its voice (`<v Speaker 2>`), and the model was asked to cite times as `[mm:ss]`. With OpenRouter's `openai/gpt-6-luna-pro` (128K context, one request) the WebVTT and Markdown Summaries were equally accurate: the WebVTT one was shorter, named fewer people, merged two actions into one and added a few correct details. The differences are the size of what a second Retry with the same format gives, so one run per format shows no gain. The WebVTT run with the small model was not compared. The experiment was reverted.

**A check pass** after the Summary was tried with a script, since removed, that repeated Takku's requests outside the app: every point that cites Transcript times was sent, six per request, with the paragraphs within 90 seconds of its times, and the model answered KEEP, FIX (who, what, decision or proposal) or DROP. Three runs with `openai/gpt-6-luna-pro` through the OpenRouter Profile, one request for the Summary (47–66 s) and four to six for the check (49–85 s): 64 points kept, 13 fixed, none dropped, and in one run the four actions cited no time and could not be checked. Luna's Summaries had nothing to drop, and the fixes were mostly worse. A few added correct details ("two or three days" of waiting, who reviews the cards the next morning). Others put "Speaker N" back where the Summary had a name, because the check sees only the passages and not who the Speakers are. Some turned actions in the checklist into sentences ("It was proposed to…"), and one brought back a misheard project name the Summary had spelled right. The three runs also differed among themselves: three or four actions, topics grouped differently, one action that only one run found.

What the small model did, for whoever tries again: Gemma 4 E4B (`ggml-org/gemma-4-E4B-it-GGUF:Q4_0` on `llama-server`, an M3 Pro with 18 GB, about 37 tokens per second):

- With *Max context* 32768 the whole Meeting went in one request. It left out a whole topic discussed around minute 25, wrote most people as "Speaker N", took a recognition error (two first names heard as one name and surname) for a person, and gave one action out of four.
- With *Max context* 12288 Takku split it into three blocks and a merge: every topic was there and there were six actions, but there were more errors. It turned proposals into decisions, reported a point on estimates as the opposite of what was said, took a joke for a topic, put the wrong owner on two actions, and copied recognition errors into them ("Gira" for Jira, "esplodenti" for "elapsed").
- A claim that looked invented was the user's own card read aloud and mis-heard ("esplodenti" for "elapsed"): check the Transcript before blaming the model.

## Consequences

- The Transcript format stays as it is. A small model gains more from what the user can already give it than from a format: names of the Speakers in the note, the topics in the personal notes, and the misheard terms as Vocabulary variants (ADR 0005).
- A lower *Max context* than the server's makes a small model cover more of a long Meeting and get more of it wrong. A 4B model is fine for short Meetings with few people; long ones with many voices need a larger model.
- Comparing formats or models needs two or three Retries each on the same Meeting, every Summary checked against the Transcript; one run each is not enough.
- The check pass is not in Takku. To try it again, on a small model where there are errors to catch, it needs: the Speakers' names and the Vocabulary in its prompt; actions left in their format; FIX only when the meaning is wrong, not to add details; and an answer for points without times.
- During these Retries copies of the note and of the Transcript appeared with the same `takku_id` and a " 1" suffix, which is not Takku's (it adds " (2)"); the user did not make them, and the Vault has Obsidian Sync on. A later Retry wrote into the Transcript copy and moved the note's link to it: `findTranscript` takes the first file with the `takku_id`, whichever it is.
